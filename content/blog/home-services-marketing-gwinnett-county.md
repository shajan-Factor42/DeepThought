## What does digital marketing for a home services business in Gwinnett County include?

For an HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical or landscaping company in Gwinnett County, digital marketing comes down to being found when something breaks and being remembered before it does.

In practice that means a short list of channels:

- **Google Search ads** for people typing "AC repair Lawrenceville" or "plumber near me" right now.
- **Google Local Services Ads**, the pay-per-lead listings with the Google Verified badge that sit above regular ads.
- **Your Google Business Profile**, with fresh photos and a steady flow of reviews. This is what shows up in the map results.
- **Facebook, Instagram and Nextdoor** to stay in front of homeowners in your service area between emergencies.
- **Retargeting** to bring back people who visited your site and didn't call, which matters most for big-ticket jobs like a roof or a new system.

If you want the bigger picture of how these fit together for any small business, our [guide to digital marketing for small business](digital-marketing-for-small-business.html) covers it. This post is about what changes when you run a home services company here.

## Why is Gwinnett County different for home services?

Gwinnett is big, busy and varied, and that changes how campaigns should be built.

It is one of Georgia's largest counties, with close to a million residents. Lawrenceville, Duluth, Suwanee, Buford, Dacula, Snellville and Lilburn each behave like their own market. Newer subdivisions around Dacula and Buford have different needs from older neighborhoods in Lilburn and Snellville, where houses are more likely to need a water heater, a panel upgrade or a new roof.

Drive time is the other big factor. A Buford plumber sending a truck to Lilburn at 5pm on I-85 or US-29 can lose the margin on the job before the van is parked. So the first rule for any Gwinnett campaign is simple: **target the ZIP codes you can serve profitably**, not the whole county. Raise bids where your best jobs come from, and cut the areas that only ever produce long drives and small tickets.

Gwinnett is also Georgia's most diverse county. In parts of Duluth, Norcross and Lilburn, ads and landing pages in Spanish or Korean can reach homeowners your competitors are ignoring.

## What does an HVAC campaign look like in Gwinnett?

HVAC marketing in Georgia follows the thermometer, and the budget should too. (For what we run for HVAC companies and what it costs, see our [HVAC marketing page](hvac-marketing-atlanta.html).)

**Summer (late May to September)** is the busy season. The first hot, humid week brings a wave of "AC not cooling" searches, and those clicks are some of the most expensive in home services because the person searching wants someone today. Campaigns should raise budgets on hot days, run call ads in the evening, and keep emergency keywords separate from routine ones so the expensive clicks get the attention they deserve.

**Winter (November to February)** brings the second peak. The first real cold snap sends people searching for "furnace repair" and "heat pump not heating." Many Gwinnett homes run heat pumps, so it pays to bid on those exact terms.

**Spring and fall** are the quiet seasons, and the best time to sell. Tune-ups, maintenance plans and system replacements are cheaper to advertise when competitors pull back. Facebook and Instagram work well here, along with retargeting to past visitors. Swap in new ads each season, because a small local audience tires of the same ad quickly. Here is [how to tell when your ads have worn out](blog-creative-fatigue.html).

## What does a plumbing campaign look like?

Plumbing is steadier than HVAC all year, but it spikes around weather and after hours.

Hard freezes bring frozen and burst pipes. Heavy rain brings sewer backups and sump pump calls. Water heaters tend to fail more in winter, when they work hardest. When a freeze is in the forecast, raise budgets on burst-pipe and emergency terms the night before.

Most plumbing searches are urgent, so a plumbing campaign is really a phone campaign. Call ads, click-to-call buttons, and someone who actually picks up after 6pm make more difference than any keyword change. Local Services Ads tend to be strong for plumbers, because the lead is a phone call from a homeowner who already chose you over the listings below.

## What does a roofing campaign look like?

Roofing demand follows the storms.

Spring and early summer are severe-weather season in north Georgia, and hail or high winds can create a burst of searches for "roof repair" and "storm damage inspection" across part of the county overnight. Late summer and fall bring the remains of tropical storms. A good roofing campaign is ready to raise budgets fast in the specific ZIP codes that got hit, and pull back when the rush is over.

Roof replacements are a slow decision. Homeowners get several quotes, call their insurance company and talk to neighbors. So roofing campaigns need more than search ads: retargeting for the weeks people spend deciding, before-and-after photos on Facebook and Instagram, and a strong presence on Nextdoor, where neighbors ask each other who they used.

Stay clear of anything that sounds like a storm chaser. Homeowners here have seen plenty of out-of-town crews after a big storm. Ads that lead with a local address, real reviews and named neighborhoods earn more trust than "free roof" promises.

## What does an electrical campaign look like?

Electrical work is less seasonal, so campaigns are built around job types instead of weather.

The steady base is repair: outlets, breakers and "power out in half the house." On top of that sit higher-value projects that are worth their own ad groups and landing pages: panel upgrades in older homes, EV charger installs in newer subdivisions, ceiling fans and lighting in spring, and whole-home generators before storm season and winter.

Each project should get its own ads and its own page. Someone searching "EV charger installation Suwanee" should land on a page about EV chargers, not your homepage.

## What does a landscaping campaign look like?

Landscaping has the clearest calendar of any trade.

**Spring (March to May)** is the peak. Sod, mulch, planting, cleanups and new installs all happen at once, so this is where most of the year's ad budget belongs. Start ads in late winter, before homeowners book someone else.

**Summer** shifts to mowing and maintenance contracts, and to irrigation. **Fall** is aeration and overseeding season for fescue lawns, plus leaf cleanup. **Winter** is quiet, which makes it a good time to promote hardscaping, outdoor lighting and design work for next spring.

Landscaping is also the most visual trade. Facebook, Instagram and Nextdoor carry more weight here than for a plumber. Real photos of your work in real Gwinnett yards beat stock images every time.

## How much should a Gwinnett home services business spend on ads?

Plan on $1,500 to $2,500 a month in ad spend per trade campaign. Below that, campaigns rarely get enough clicks to learn what works.

For context, home and home improvement businesses paid about $8.33 per click and about $91 per lead on Google Ads in 2026, according to [WordStream's 2026 benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2026-google-ads-benchmarks). ## What should you measure?

Measure booked jobs, not clicks.

Track phone calls from ads and from your Business Profile, form fills, and which of those became paying jobs. Then work out your cost per booked job for each trade and each part of the county. That number tells you where to put next month's budget. A campaign with cheap clicks and no booked jobs is not working, however good the report looks.

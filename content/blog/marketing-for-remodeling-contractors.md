## What marketing works for remodeling contractors?

Remodeling is a slow, expensive decision, so the marketing has to last as long as the decision does. That means two jobs:

1. **Catch the homeowners who are ready now** with Google search ads and a strong Google Business Profile.
2. **Stay in front of everyone still deciding** with retargeting, streaming TV, social ads and email, for months.

Most remodelers only do the first. That works for repairs. It doesn't work for a five-figure kitchen, where the homeowner may visit your site in January and sign with someone in September. ## How long do homeowners take to decide?

Longer than most contractors plan for. The [2026 Houzz & Home Study](https://www.woodworkingnetwork.com/design/home-renovation-activity-steady-2025-2026-sees-decline-plans-houzz-study-finds), a survey of more than 20,000 US homeowners, found:

| Project | Average planning time | Average build time |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen | 9.5 months | 5.8 months |
| Closet | 6.6 months | 4.3 months |
| Home office | 6.1 months | 3.4 months |

Planning takes longer than building. The same study found the median renovation spend was $20,000 in 2025, and 91% of renovating homeowners hired a professional.

It's a big market, too: Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies projects US homeowners will spend about $522 billion on improvements and repairs in 2026.

So the contractor who's still in front of the homeowner in month eight has a real advantage over the one they found in month one and forgot.

## Step 1: Be found when they're ready to talk

- **Search ads** for project terms with intent: "kitchen remodel contractor near me", "bathroom remodeling [your town]", "basement finishing cost". Split them by project, because a kitchen and a bathroom are worth very different amounts.
- **Google Business Profile** with real project photos, updated often, and steady reviews. Remodel buyers read reviews closely. See [why your reviews do more of the selling than your ads](blog-reviews-do-the-selling.html).
- **A page for each main project type** that shows finished work, a rough timeline and how a consultation works.

## Step 2: Give them a reason to raise their hand early

Most people planning a remodel aren't ready to book a consultation. Give them something smaller to say yes to:

- A **kitchen or bath planning guide** they can download.
- A **cost guide** for your area with honest ranges.
- A **design ideas email series** from real projects you've done.

Each one turns an anonymous visitor into someone you can follow up with by email.

## Step 3: Stay in front of them for months

This is where most remodelers lose jobs they never knew they were in the running for.

**Retargeting.** Show your finished projects, reviews and a consultation offer to people who visited your site, for 60 to 180 days. Change the ads every few weeks so they don't go stale.

**Streaming TV.** Ads on streaming services, targeted to the ZIP codes or radius you serve, build your name with homeowners long before they search. It works best with a budget that can run steadily for a few months. Our guide to [what streaming TV advertising costs](blog-streaming-tv-what-it-costs.html) has the numbers.

**Social ads.** Before-and-after photos and short project videos on Facebook and Instagram, shown to homeowners in your area.

**Email follow-up.** A simple monthly email (a finished project, a planning tip, a seasonal note) keeps you in mind for every guide download and past quote. It's the cheapest channel you'll run. See [why email still pays](blog-email-still-pays.html).

## Step 4: Measure booked consultations, not clicks

Clicks are cheap to count and easy to misread. For a remodeler, the numbers that matter are:

| Measure | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Booked consultations | The first real sign of a project |
| Signed projects | What actually pays |
| Cost per signed project | Total marketing spend ÷ signed projects |
| Lead source, first and last | Which channel found them, and which one closed them |

Two things make this work:

- **Record the first source of every lead**, not just the last. A homeowner who first saw you on streaming TV and later searched your name should credit both. Otherwise, slow awareness channels look worthless and get cut.
- **Review on a longer cycle.** A remodel lead can take three to nine months to sign. Judge awareness channels on a quarter or two, not a week.

## A simple monthly budget shape

Every business is different, but a common starting shape for a remodeler looks like this:

- **40–50%** on search ads and Local Services Ads, to catch people ready to talk.
- **25–35%** on retargeting and social ads, to stay in front of visitors.
- **The rest** on streaming TV in your best ZIP codes, once search and retargeting are running well.
- **Email** costs very little, so run it from day one.

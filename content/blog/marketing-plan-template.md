## Why one page?

A plan only works if you read it again. Thirty-page plans get written once and filed. One page can sit on your desk or pinned in your office, and you can review it in 30 minutes.

One page also makes you choose. If a channel or a goal doesn't fit, that's usually a sign it isn't the priority right now.

## Box 1: Goal

Write **one number and a date**. Examples:

- 40 booked jobs a month by March 31
- 25 new patients a month by June 30
- $30,000 a month in online orders by the holidays

"More customers" or "grow the brand" can't be checked, so you'll never know if the plan worked. Pick the number that pays the bills: booked jobs, new customers or sales.

## Box 2: Audience

Three lines:

- **Who** buys from you. Be specific: "homeowners with older AC units", not "everyone".
- **Where** they are. Towns, ZIP codes or a radius you can actually serve.
- **Why they buy.** What happened that made them look for you? A broken system, a new baby, a season, a move.

The "why" decides your message. The "where" decides your targeting and stops you paying for clicks from people you can't serve.

## Box 3: Budget and split

Decide the **total monthly ad spend** first. Then split it across channels, and write down any management or agency fee separately so you can see how much actually reaches customers. Our post on the [working media ratio](blog-working-media-ratio.html) shows how to work that out.

Here's an example split for a made-up home services company spending $3,000 a month:

| Channel | % of spend | $ / month | Job it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google search ads | 50% | $1,500 | Be found when someone searches now |
| Google Local Services Ads | 20% | $600 | Pay-per-lead calls with the Google badge |
| Facebook and Instagram | 20% | $600 | Seasonal offer to the service area |
| Retargeting | 10% | $300 | Bring back people who visited the site |

The right split depends on your business. A shop that sells online will lean more on shopping and social ads. A dentist may lean on search and reviews. For real budget ranges, see [how much marketing costs for a small business](blog-digital-marketing-cost-small-business-gwinnett-county.html).

## Box 4: Channels, and what each one is for

Start with **two or three channels**, each with one clear job:

- **To be found:** Google search ads, Local Services Ads, your Google Business Profile.
- **To be remembered:** social ads, streaming TV, display ads in your area.
- **To bring people back:** retargeting and email.

A small budget spread across eight channels does little in each. It's better to do three well, then add one when the first three are working.

## Box 5: What to measure

Pick **three numbers** you'll check every month, with a target for each and where you'll find it:

| Measure | Example target | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Booked jobs or sales | 40 a month | Your job software, POS or call log |
| Cost per booked job | $75 | Ad spend ÷ booked jobs |
| Calls or leads from ads | 120 a month | Call tracking, Google Ads, form emails |

Clicks, impressions and likes are useful for the people running the ads, but they don't pay the bills. Judge the plan on the first two rows.

## Box 6: Review date

Write the **first review date** and put it in your calendar today. Then decide how often you'll review after that (monthly works for most small businesses) and who owns it.

At each review, ask three questions:

1. Did we hit the goal? If not, which number missed?
2. Which channel brought the cheapest booked jobs? Should it get more budget?
3. Did anything change in the business (a season, a new service, staffing) that the plan needs to reflect?

Then update the page. It takes 30 minutes, and it's where the plan earns its keep.

## Common mistakes

- **No number in the goal.** If you can't count it, you can't review it.
- **Too many channels.** Spreading a small budget thin is the most common way plans fail.
- **Measuring clicks.** A cheap click that never books is not a win.
- **No review.** A plan that's never reviewed quietly turns back into guessing.

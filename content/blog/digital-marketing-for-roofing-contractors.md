## What digital marketing works for roofing contractors?

The digital marketing that works best for roofing contractors reaches homeowners at the moment they need a roofer: **Google search ads, Google Local Services Ads and a complete Google Business Profile.** After a storm, add **neighborhood targeting** and **retargeting** to win the replacement jobs that follow.

Roofing is different from most trades in one way: demand arrives in bursts. A hailstorm can create more calls in a week than a quiet month, and the roofers who catch them are the ones who were ready before it hit. This guide to digital marketing for roofers is about being ready. For the wider picture across trades, see our guide to [where contractor marketing budgets should go](blog-digital-marketing-for-contractors.html).

## Plan for storm season before it starts

The National Weather Service says Georgia's **peak severe thunderstorm season is March, April and May**, with a second peak in some years in September and October. Severe storms can still happen in any month.

So the work happens in the quiet months:

- **Build storm campaigns now and pause them.** Search ads for "hail damage roof repair", "roof leak repair" and "storm damage roof inspection", written and approved, so you can switch them on the day a storm passes.
- **Get Local Services Ads verified.** Google's background and license checks can take a few weeks. Do it before you need it.
- **Fix the phone.** Most roofing leads are calls. If calls go to voicemail after a storm, you are paying for leads that go to the next roofer. Plan who answers, and schedule ads for when someone will.
- **Have a landing page for storm damage.** One page that explains what an inspection includes, what happens next and how fast you can get there. Not your home page.

## Draw a service radius that pays

Most wasted roofing spend comes from showing ads where your crews can't work profitably.

- **Start from your yard, not the map.** Draw the area by drive time from where your crews start each day, and leave out places you'd turn down.
- **Use ZIP codes for control.** ZIP code targeting lets you raise bids where your best jobs come from and cut areas that only bring small repairs.
- **Tighten it after a storm.** When hail hits a few ZIP codes, raise budgets there for two to three weeks and leave the rest of your area at normal levels.
- **Check the location setting.** Target people who are in or regularly in your area, not people who are only "interested in" it. That default quietly sends clicks from out of town. We explain the difference in [how radius targeting really works](blog-geofencing-how-it-works.html).

## The leads worth paying for

Not every roofing lead is worth the same money. Ranked from most to least valuable for most roofers:

| Lead source | Why it's worth paying for (or not) |
|---|---|
| **Your own search ads** | The homeowner searched, found you and called you. Nobody else gets that call. |
| **Google Local Services Ads** | Pay per lead, not per click, above the regular ads, with a Google badge. Invalid leads can be disputed. |
| **Google Business Profile and reviews** | Free, and decides whether you show in the map results. Reviews after every job compound. |
| **Retargeting** | Brings back homeowners who visited your site, often the ones comparing quotes for a replacement. |
| **Shared lead lists** | The same homeowner sold to several roofers. You compete on speed and price for every job. |

Split **repair** and **replacement** into separate campaigns. A leak repair and a full replacement are worth very different amounts, and mixed together, cheaper repair clicks eat the budget meant for replacement searches.

Roofing clicks often cost more than the home services average of about $8.33 a click on Google Ads ([WordStream, 2026](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2026-google-ads-benchmarks)), because one replacement job is worth thousands of dollars. That makes it more important, not less, to know which clicks turn into jobs.

## After the storm: what to run

Once a storm passes and your crews can take work:

1. **Switch on the paused storm campaigns** in the ZIP codes that were hit.
2. **Raise Local Services Ads budgets** for those areas.
3. **Turn on display or streaming TV ads in the hit neighborhoods** with a simple, honest offer, such as a free roof inspection. Homeowners who haven't checked their roof yet will see your name first.
4. **Retarget everyone who visited your storm page** for the next few weeks. Replacement decisions take time, often waiting on an insurance adjuster.
5. **Ask every finished job for a review.** After a storm, homeowners read reviews closely because they've heard about storm chasers. See [why your reviews do more selling than your ads](blog-reviews-do-the-selling.html).

## Stay on the right side of storm rules

Storm advertising draws attention from regulators because of out-of-town "storm chasers". Rules vary by state, so check yours with an attorney. A few common ones:

- **In Georgia, roofing contractors can't represent or negotiate a homeowner's insurance claim, or advertise that they will** (O.C.G.A. § 10-1-393.12). Homeowners also get a window to cancel a roofing contract if their insurer denies the claim.
- **Don't promise to cover or waive the deductible.** Many states treat that as insurance fraud.
- **Keep claims honest.** Say what you do ("free inspection", "licensed and insured in Georgia") rather than promising what insurance will pay.

Ads that stay inside these lines also convert better, because they sound like a local roofer, not a storm chaser.

## How to know it's working

Track every call and form by source, then count booked jobs, not leads. Divide each channel's spend by the jobs it booked. A channel with expensive leads can still be the best one if it books replacements.

Review it monthly, and after every storm. The storm weeks tell you which channels to lean on next time.

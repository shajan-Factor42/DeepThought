## What digital marketing should a contractor run?

Start with the channels that reach homeowners when they need a contractor right now: **Google search ads, Google Local Services Ads and your Google Business Profile.** Add social ads, retargeting and streaming TV once those are producing calls, to sell the bigger jobs people plan ahead for.

That order matters more than the total budget. A contractor who spreads $2,000 a month across six channels usually gets less than one who puts it into two channels and runs them well. ## The channels, ranked by how close they are to a booked job

| Channel | What it does for a contractor | When to add it |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Business Profile** | Decides whether you show in the map results. Free. | Day one, and keep it current |
| **Google search ads** | Catches people searching "roof repair near me" or "AC not cooling" | First paid channel |
| **Google Local Services Ads** | Pay-per-lead listings above search ads, with a Google verification badge | Alongside search, if your trade qualifies |
| **Retargeting** | Brings back people who visited your site and didn't call | Once your site gets steady traffic |
| **Facebook, Instagram and Nextdoor ads** | Sells planned jobs: replacements, maintenance plans, remodels, seasonal offers | Once search is producing calls |
| **Streaming TV and online video** | Builds your name before people need you | Once the basics work and you want to grow |

**Search ads and Local Services Ads** do the heavy lifting because they reach people with a job ready to go. Home and home improvement businesses paid about **$8.33 per click and $91 per lead** on Google Ads in 2026, according to [WordStream's 2026 benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2026-google-ads-benchmarks), and emergency searches cost more than that.

**Social, Nextdoor and streaming TV ads** won't catch the burst pipe at 11pm. They work on the homeowner who will need a new roof, a new system or a kitchen remodel in the next few months, and they make your name familiar when that day comes.

## Where to spend, trade by trade

Each trade has its own mix and its own busy season. A starting point:

| Trade | Lead with | Busy season (Southeast) | Worth adding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HVAC** | Search + Local Services Ads | Cooling late May–Sept; heating Nov–Feb | Social for tune-ups and maintenance plans |
| **Plumbing** | Search + Local Services Ads | Year-round, spikes in freezes | Retargeting for repipes and water heaters |
| **Roofing** | Search, plus fast response after storms | Spring and after major storms | Geofencing storm-hit neighborhoods |
| **Electrical** | Search + Local Services Ads | Year-round | Social for generators, panels and EV chargers |
| **Landscaping** | Social + search | Spring and fall | Nextdoor and seasonal offers |
| **Remodeling** | Search + retargeting | Late winter planning into spring | Streaming TV and email for long decisions |

## Where contractor ad budgets leak

Most wasted contractor spend comes from a few repeat problems:

- **Targeting the whole metro.** If your crews cover 20 miles, ads shown 40 miles away buy clicks you can't serve. Draw the area around the ZIP codes you can reach profitably, and raise bids where your best jobs come from.
- **One campaign for every job type.** Emergency repairs, replacements and maintenance are worth very different amounts. Mixed together, cheap clicks eat the budget meant for the valuable ones.
- **Calls going to voicemail.** Emergency ads that run after hours with nobody answering are paying for leads to go to a competitor. Schedule them for when someone picks up.
- **No call tracking.** Most contractor leads are phone calls. Without tracking, nobody knows which channel produced the job, and budget decisions become guesses.
- **Shared leads in place of your own ads.** Lead websites sell the same homeowner to several contractors. Your own ads send the call only to you, and they get cheaper per job as the campaigns learn.
- **Ads that never change.** The same ad running for months to the same small local audience gets more expensive over time. See [why your ad creative stops working](blog-creative-fatigue.html).

## How to split a contractor marketing budget

*This is an illustration, not a rule. Your mix depends on your trade, area and season.*

A contractor with **$3,000 a month** in ad spend, running in one metro area, might start like this:

- **Google search ads: about 50%.** The searches closest to a booked job.
- **Local Services Ads: about 25%.** Pay-per-lead, above the search ads.
- **Retargeting and social: about 25%.** Bring back site visitors and sell planned jobs.

Hold streaming TV until the first three are producing calls at a cost per job you're happy with. Then shift budget toward whatever is producing booked jobs most cheaply, and lean into each trade's busy season.

## What to measure

Clicks and leads are easy to count and easy to be fooled by. The number that matters is **cost per booked job**, by channel:

1. Track every call and form, with a separate tracking number or source for each channel.
2. Mark which leads became booked jobs, and what each job was worth.
3. Divide each channel's spend by the jobs it booked.

Review it monthly. A channel with expensive leads can still be the best one if those leads book large jobs.

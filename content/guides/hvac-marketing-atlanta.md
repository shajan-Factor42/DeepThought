## What HVAC marketing includes

HVAC marketing is everything that gets your phone ringing when a homeowner's AC quits in July or the heat pump stops in January, and keeps your name in front of them the rest of the year.

For an HVAC company in metro Atlanta, that comes down to a short list:

- **Google Search ads** for "AC repair Lawrenceville", "furnace repair near me" and "heat pump not heating".
- **Google Local Services Ads**, the pay-per-lead listings with the Google Verified badge that sit above every other result.
- **Your Google Business Profile**, with current hours, photos and a steady flow of reviews. This is what decides the map results.
- **Facebook, Instagram and Nextdoor** to sell tune-ups, maintenance plans and new systems before something breaks.
- **Retargeting** to bring back homeowners who looked at a replacement and didn't call. A new system is a big decision, and people take weeks to make it.
- **Streaming TV and online video**, once the basics are working, so people know your name before they need you.

Most HVAC owners end up with a different vendor, login and invoice for each of these. **DeepThought puts all of it in one place.** We plan, buy, launch and manage your ads across 22 advertising products, and send you one plain-English report on what came in.

## Which HVAC jobs are worth paying for

Not every click is worth the same. A good HVAC campaign splits jobs by what they're worth and how urgent they are, and pays accordingly.

| Job type | What people search | How to run it |
|---|---|---|
| **Emergency repair** | "AC not cooling", "no heat", "emergency HVAC" | Highest bids, call ads, evenings and weekends. Only worth it if someone answers the phone. |
| **System replacement** | "new AC unit cost", "heat pump replacement" | Search plus retargeting for the weeks people spend deciding. Highest value per job. |
| **Tune-ups and maintenance plans** | "AC tune-up", "furnace maintenance" | Lower bids, social ads in spring and fall. Plans turn one-time customers into yearly ones. |
| **Financing** | "HVAC financing", "AC replacement payment plan" | Pair with replacement ads. Removes the main reason people delay. |

Keeping these separate is what stops cheap tune-up clicks from eating the budget meant for replacements, or emergency calls from going to voicemail at 8pm.

## What HVAC marketing costs

Every HVAC marketing budget has two parts.

**The ad spend.** This is what Google, Meta and the other platforms charge to show your ads. Home and home improvement businesses paid about $8.33 per click and $91 per lead on Google Ads in 2026, according to [WordStream's 2026 benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2026-google-ads-benchmarks). Emergency HVAC searches cost more than that. Plan on $1,500 to $2,500 a month per campaign. Below that, a campaign rarely gets enough clicks to learn what works.

**The help.** This is what you pay someone to run the ads. Agency retainers commonly run $1,500 to $4,000 a month for the fee alone, and percentage-of-spend deals often take 30% to 40% of your media. ## How to judge any HVAC marketing company

Ask these before you sign with anyone, including us:

1. **How much of my monthly bill goes to the ads themselves?** Get a dollar figure, not a percentage of a package.
2. **Will I own the Google Ads and Meta accounts?** You should have Admin access on your own login.
3. **Do you separate emergency, replacement and maintenance campaigns?** One campaign for everything wastes money.
4. **How do you track calls?** Most HVAC leads are phone calls. If calls aren't tracked, the reports are guesses.
5. **What happens in the first 30 days?** You should hear a specific plan, not "we'll learn as we go".
6. **How often do you change the ads themselves?** Ads wear out with the same homeowners, so new creative should go in every season. Here is [why ad creative stops working](blog-creative-fatigue.html).
